The Taj Mahal

"A teardrop on the cheek of eternity" — Rabindranath Tagore

The Taj Mahal is one of the most iconic monuments in the world, located in Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India.

History

Commissioned by: Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan.

Purpose: Built as a mausoleum for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, who died during childbirth in 1631.

Construction Period: 1632–1653 (approximately 22 years).

Architects: Ustad Ahmad Lahori is credited as the chief architect, supported by skilled artisans, calligraphers, and craftsmen from across the empire.

Cost: Estimated at 32 million rupees at the time — equivalent to billions today.

Architecture

Style: A masterpiece blending Persian, Islamic, and Indian Mughal styles.

Materials: White marble from Makrana, Rajasthan, inlaid with lapis lazuli, jade, crystal, and turquoise.

Complex Layout:

  • Main Mausoleum: Central structure with a large "onion dome" and four smaller domes.
  • Minarets: Four minarets framing the mausoleum, each leaning slightly outward for structural safety.
  • Gardens: Charbagh-style layout divided into four quadrants by water channels.

Did You Know?

The Taj Mahal changes color throughout the day — pinkish at dawn, white in the afternoon, and golden under moonlight.

Significance

  • Symbol of Love: Represents Shah Jahan's eternal devotion to Mumtaz Mahal.
  • UNESCO World Heritage Site: Designated in 1983 for its cultural and historical importance.
  • New Seven Wonders: Listed among the New Seven Wonders of the World.

Interesting Facts

  • The walls feature Quranic calligraphy using a style that appears uniform in size from ground level.
  • Shah Jahan reportedly planned a black marble mausoleum for himself across the Yamuna River — never built.
  • The Taj Mahal attracts 6–8 million visitors annually.

Visiting the Taj Mahal

Best Time: October to March; sunrise and sunset offer the most stunning views.

Night Viewing: Available on full moon nights and two days before/after (except Fridays and Ramadan). Advance booking required.

Nearby Attractions

  • Agra Fort: A UNESCO World Heritage Site and former Mughal royal residence.
  • Fatehpur Sikri: Historic city built by Emperor Akbar, 40 km from Agra.
  • Mehtab Bagh: Garden complex with stunning views of the Taj across the Yamuna River.
The Taj Mahal remains a timeless symbol of art, love, and history, drawing millions of visitors from around the globe.
