Charminar

The Jewel of Hyderabad, standing tall since 1591

The Charminar is a historic and iconic monument located in the heart of Hyderabad, Telangana, India — one of the most recognized landmarks of the city and a symbol of its rich cultural heritage.

History

Commissioned by: Sultan Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, the fifth ruler of the Qutb Shahi dynasty.

Year of Construction: 1591.

Purpose: Built to commemorate the end of a deadly plague and to mark the founding of Hyderabad as the new capital of the Qutb Shahi dynasty, shifting from Golconda.

Name: "Charminar" translates to "Four Towers" in Urdu, derived from the Persian words Char (four) and Minar (tower).

Architecture

Style: A fine example of Indo-Islamic architecture with Persian influences.

Structure:

  • Four grand arches facing the four cardinal directions.
  • Four minarets, each 56 meters (184 feet) tall, divided into four stories.
  • A spiral staircase of 149 steps leads to the top floor.
  • The top floor houses a mosque — one of the oldest in Hyderabad.

Materials: Granite, limestone, mortar, and pulverized marble.

Design: Arches and domes decorated with stucco and floral motifs showcasing Qutb Shahi-era craftsmanship.

Architectural Marvel

The four minarets are said to represent the first four caliphs of Islam, and each leans slightly outward — an intentional design to protect the main structure in case of collapse.

Significance

  • Cultural Icon: Often called the "Arc de Triomphe of the East," the Charminar is synonymous with Hyderabad's identity.
  • Historical Importance: A testament to the Qutb Shahi dynasty's architectural and cultural achievements.
  • Religious Use: The mosque on the top floor remains active for prayers to this day.

Interesting Facts

  • Originally built at the center of Hyderabad, with the city planned in a grid pattern around it.
  • A rumored underground tunnel connecting it to Golconda Fort has never been confirmed.
  • The monument is illuminated at night, creating a striking visual spectacle.
  • The surrounding Charminar Bazaar is famous for Laad Bazaar (lacquer bangles) and Pather Gatti (pearls).

Marketplace Wonder

The Charminar Bazaar is one of Hyderabad's oldest markets, renowned for pearls, bangles, and traditional Hyderabadi attire — especially the exquisite lacquer bangles of Laad Bazaar.

Visiting the Charminar

Best Time to Visit: Early morning or late evening, when the weather is pleasant and the monument is beautifully lit.

Nearby Attractions: Mecca Masjid, Chowmahalla Palace, and Salar Jung Museum.

The Charminar remains a timeless symbol of Hyderabad's history, culture, and architectural grandeur
